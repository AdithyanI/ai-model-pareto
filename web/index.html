Model selection

Two charts, one object

Intelligence, cost and speed get published as two flat scatter plots. They are not two charts. They are two camera angles on the same three-dimensional object, and once you rotate it, a set of genuinely optimal models appears that neither flat view can show.

What the flat charts cannot say

A model is worth considering when nothing else is at least as good on every axis you care about. On a flat chart that test only runs on two axes, so a model can be discarded for being slow by a chart that never measured its price, and discarded for being expensive by a chart that never measured its speed. Judge all three together and it survives.

That is the amber set above. Those configurations are not compromises. They are the options you can only see by rotating the object, and they are exactly the ones a two-chart reading throws away.

How to read it honestly

Points are model configurations on a specific host, because price and latency belong to the host rather than the model. Cost and time use logarithmic axes; the field spans orders of magnitude and a linear axis would crush everything cheap into the margin. Nothing is interpolated: the space between points contains no models, only empty space.
